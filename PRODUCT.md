# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is a college evaluator or computer-science student who understands graphs and algorithms but may know nothing about Bitcoin or the Lightning Network. They use the application in a classroom, project demonstration, or self-guided browser session to understand a routing problem, inspect the algorithm's decisions, and reproduce measurable experiments.

## Product Purpose

Google Maps for Lightning Payments is an educational, deterministic simulation of routing a payment through a distributed network. It helps a newcomer see how route length, fees, usable capacity, latency, reliability, and failures compete. Success means a visitor understands the general routing problem within one minute, the non-trivial optimization within three minutes, and the measurable engineering results within five minutes.

## Positioning

It makes a complex payment-routing problem understandable by turning it into a familiar, explorable map with plain-language explanations for every algorithmic choice.

## Brand Personality

Clear, credible, and curious. The interface should feel calm enough to teach, precise enough to trust in an engineering evaluation, and responsive enough to reward exploration. Its emotional goal is confident understanding—not excitement about cryptocurrency.

## Anti-references

This must not resemble a cryptocurrency trading dashboard or a toy student project. Avoid giant Bitcoin logos, spinning coins, hacker imagery, excessive neon, generic crypto gradients, blockchain cubes, ornamental glassmorphism, generic Tailwind card grids, unsupported AI claims, or dense blockchain internals before the routing concept is understood.

## Design Principles

- Teach in familiar language before introducing Lightning terminology.
- Make every algorithmic outcome explainable in normal prose and accessible outside the graph.
- Let the simulated network respond visibly and deterministically to user choices.
- Use map and journey conventions to orient the user; never make the graph feel like decorative data art.
- Label synthetic estimates, constraints, and limitations with academic honesty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All essential route data must be available as semantic text rather than only through color or graph position. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, touch targets, reduced motion, high-contrast text, color-independent status cues, and responsive layouts from 360px through large desktop screens.
